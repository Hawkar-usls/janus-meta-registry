# DRAFT — FBI / Foreign Influence Task Force — v2.0

**Status:** NOT SENT

To the Federal Bureau of Investigation / Foreign Influence Task Force,

I am submitting an unclassified early-warning package concerning a possible future convergence of capabilities that are already independently documented: Russia's repeated control of information-distribution infrastructure during conflicts; Russia's centrally managed TSPU public-network control architecture; exported Russian SORM/DPI surveillance technologies; and Russian government-linked foreign malign influence operations using fake personas and bot farms.

I am **not** alleging that Russia currently operates a synchronized global TSPU network, nor that the current Threads activity described below has been proven to be a Russian state operation. Those claims would exceed the available evidence. I am asking whether the missing technical and counterintelligence links merit collection.

## 1. Historical and technical foundation

Public evidence shows a recurring distribution-control mechanism. In conflicts involving Russia or Russian-backed forces, broadcast and communications distribution has repeatedly been treated as strategic terrain: broadcast infrastructure was neutralized in Chechnya; Ukrainian television/radio distribution was seized and replaced with Russian/aligned sources in Crimea and parts of Donetsk and Luhansk in 2014; and after the 2022 invasion, occupied Ukrainian Internet networks were rerouted through Russian upstreams, causing users to inherit Russian network-control and censorship conditions.

Independent research now shows that Russia possesses a much more programmable domestic capability. Censored Planet characterizes TSPU as pervasive, in-path and centrally controlled across privately owned Russian ISPs. FOCI 2026 documented large-scale SNI-dependent QUIC censorship, demonstrating flexible protocol policy. Human Rights Watch has documented escalating Russian network manipulation, isolation and shutdown capability.

By 2025-2026 Russia also publicly operated a mobile **deny-except-allowlist** regime: ordinary mobile Internet can be restricted while selected approved services remain available. Operationally, this is more important than simple propaganda. A controller can reduce external communications and external information reach while preserving selected domestic services.

Russian SORM/surveillance technology has also proliferated abroad. Recorded Future / Insikt Group identified multiple Russian providers and foreign telecom customers and assessed that Russian-component foreign systems may create vendor/Russian access risk, including backdoor/manufacturer-access concerns, while explicitly stating that the degree of Moscow's actual access is unclear. I therefore treat foreign remote control as an unresolved counterintelligence question, not a fact.

## 2. Confirmed Russian fake-persona / bot-farm precedent

The FBI and DOJ have already documented a Russian government-operated AI-enhanced bot farm that used 968 social-media accounts and fictitious personas to distribute narratives supporting Russian government objectives in the United States and abroad. DOJ records state that development involved RT personnel and later Kremlin approval and an FSB-linked organization.

Meta has separately attributed multiple coordinated inauthentic behavior networks to Russia or actors associated with the Internet Research Agency. Relevant tactics included fake accounts, impersonation, coordinated following/liking/commenting to increase engagement, and audience building through initially non-political subjects including fashion, celebrity gossip, lifestyle and famous personalities.

This precedent matters because an influence operation does not need to begin with political content. It can first build reach, familiarity and trust.

## 3. Current Threads lead — celebrity reply bait / audience seeding

In March 2026, a highly visible Threads wave formed around Russian celebrities including Nikolai Baskov and Dima Bilan. Independent public reporting confirms that the celebrities or their account managers repeatedly replied to apparently random users; the behavior became a platform-wide meme, including jokes that a user could post a random thought and soon receive a reply from Baskov or Bilan. Contemporary users also publicly suspected PR/SMM management of the accounts.

I have independently observed a more specific pattern that I cannot verify from public search alone: a cluster of accounts presenting as teenage/young women, approximately 15-18+ in persona, repeatedly promoting Baskov and Bilan around the hook that if users mention the celebrity, the celebrity may personally answer them. The apparent replies are then used as screenshots/social proof, encouraging more users to repeat the behavior and creating a flashmob-like engagement loop.

I am **not asking the FBI to accept my attribution of those accounts**. Their claimed age, gender, authenticity and operator remain unresolved. I am presenting the pattern because it closely matches already documented influence tactics: fictitious personas, engagement amplification, non-political audience seeding and manufactured social proof.

There is also a relevant psychological reason to take the hook seriously without assuming that every user is persuadable. Peer-reviewed research with adolescents found that direct social-media interactions with celebrities — such as replies or retweets — were associated with stronger parasocial relationships. A 2026 longitudinal study further found that social-media influencers can function as perceived "digital super peers" for adolescents in socio-political information environments. This does not prove that the current Threads campaign targets minors, but it makes a deliberate celebrity-reply mechanism a testable audience-influence vector.

## 4. JUXTAPOSE — why I mention it only to the FBI

I previously submitted to DARPA an independent research package called **JUXTAPOSE: Exact-Backed Adaptive Search for Resilient Communications in DDIL Environments**. It is defensive, transport-agnostic communications-resilience research. Its core rule is: **"learn where to look; never learn what is true."** Only fresh end-to-end measurement establishes current connectivity; stale evidence, failures and UNKNOWN states are preserved rather than hidden.

I mention JUXTAPOSE only here because it helped me recognize the inverse threat model. A defensive system reasons about reachable paths, shared failure domains, stale evidence and route diversity. A hostile centrally controlled network-policy layer can potentially create those failure domains deliberately for public-network-dependent users. JUXTAPOSE is not proof of Russian behavior and I am not presenting it as an offensive technique.

## 5. The combined risk hypothesis

The risk is not simply "Russian propaganda."

It is a possible two-lane system:

**Lane A — network control:** deny, allowlist, filter or reroute external communications and information paths.

**Lane B — platform influence:** fake personas, bot farms, celebrity/lifestyle audience seeding, engagement amplification and later narrative delivery inside the platforms that remain reachable.

The combination could be substantially more powerful than either layer alone: external/open communications become less available while selected local content and socially amplified narratives gain a larger relative share of attention.

Again, **I have not established that these two lanes are currently coupled in one operation.** That is the central collection question.

## 6. Evidence threshold I would require before attribution

I would not attribute an event to Russia based on a celebrity trend, a network outage, Russian-origin equipment, similar narratives or suspicious personas alone. A serious escalation should require multiple independent bridges such as:

- platform evidence that supposedly unrelated personas share operator/device/payment/network infrastructure;
- overlap with previously attributed RT/IRA/FSB/Kremlin-linked influence infrastructure;
- repeated text, media, URL and posting-time synchronization beyond matched commercial-SMM controls;
- a transition from audience-building content into coordinated political/war narratives;
- foreign-vantage selective loss of U.S./open services while approved/local services remain available;
- reproducible DPI/TSPU-like behavior or Russian-origin control-stack evidence;
- path/device localization and healthy U.S.-side controls;
- and evidence of shared Russian vendor/state command or remote administration before cross-border TSPU attribution.

If the Threads accounts are ordinary independent fans, if the celebrity activity is standard commercial SMM, or if platform telemetry shows no common operator, that should falsify the bot-farm interpretation.

## 7. Why this merits FBI review

The FBI leads the Foreign Influence Task Force and has already disrupted Russian fictitious-persona and bot-farm infrastructure. The specific questions here — covert account operation, fake personas, possible platform manipulation, Russian state or intelligence links, vendor remote access and U.S./Western audience targeting — fit a counterintelligence / foreign influence review more directly than a general political assessment.

I have assembled an open-source package with confidence labels, historical controls, negative controls, technical TSPU literature, foreign SORM/DPI deployment evidence, current Threads observations, peer-reviewed audience research, and explicit corrections where our own hypothesis weakened.

I am not asking the FBI to accept the hypothesis. I am asking whether the **external telecom control-plane question** and the **current persona/reply-bait attribution question** are already understood or merit collection.
