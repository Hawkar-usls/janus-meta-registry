**Suggested subject:** Threads Integrity Review Request: Possible Coordinated Inauthentic Amplification Around Russian Pro-War Celebrities

To Meta Integrity / Threat Disruption / Threads Safety,

I am an independent researcher in Ukraine and I am asking Meta to review a current Threads pattern that may involve coordinated inauthentic behavior, artificial amplification, or deceptive SMM activity. I am **not** asking Meta to remove people because of their political views or because of the content they post. I am asking for a behavior-based review of a network pattern that appears similar to categories Meta has previously investigated under Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior (CIB), inauthentic amplification, and foreign/government interference.

## Current observable pattern

In 2026 I observed a recurring Threads pattern centered on Russian celebrities Nikolay Baskov and Dima Bilan. The public-facing mechanic is simple and viral: users mention or address one of the celebrities, the celebrity account replies, and the reply is then used as social proof encouraging more users to repeat the behavior in the hope that the celebrity will answer them too.

A broader Baskov/Bilan/Leps reply-wave on Threads has been publicly noticeable and discussed as a viral celebrity phenomenon. My concern is more specific: I observed a cluster of accounts presenting themselves as very young women / girls, approximately late-teen or young-adult personas, repeatedly participating in or amplifying this reply-bait mechanic around Baskov and Bilan.

I cannot independently verify the real ages or identities of these accounts, and I am **not** claiming they are actual minors. I am also not claiming, without Meta telemetry, that they are bots or that the Russian state operates them. I am asking Meta to determine whether the accounts are authentic, whether they share operators/devices/network infrastructure, whether their activity is coordinated, and whether they are being used to create artificial engagement or audience seeding.

## Why this pattern merits an integrity review

Meta's own CIB framework defines coordinated inauthentic behavior around networks that rely on fake accounts to mislead people about who is behind an operation or what it is doing. Meta has also documented inauthentic behavior centered on artificial amplification, fake engagement, and misleading people about the popularity of content.

There is relevant precedent involving Russian-linked operations. Meta has previously removed Russia-linked networks on Facebook and Instagram that used fake personas and coordinated behavior. In March 2020, Meta described a Russia-linked network that was still in the audience-building phase and used non-political content such as fashion, celebrity gossip, lifestyle, and famous personalities to grow audiences before or alongside influence activity. Meta has also documented Russia-linked networks using fake accounts to pose as locals, journalists, ordinary citizens, and other personas.

This precedent does **not** prove that the current Baskov/Bilan Threads pattern is Russian state-directed. It does establish that celebrity/lifestyle audience seeding and fake-persona engagement are legitimate threat-investigation categories for Meta.

## Why the celebrity / youth-facing dimension matters

Nikolay Baskov publicly supported Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine and later publicly supported Russian military personnel. Dima Bilan has also publicly engaged in support activities around Russia's war and occupied Donetsk. These facts are independently documented.

The strongest direct youth-facing connection is **Dima Bilan**, who has served repeatedly as a mentor on Russia's television program "The Voice Kids" ("Golos.Deti"). Channel One currently describes him as its most experienced mentor in the children's competition. This gives him a well-established parasocial relationship with younger audiences.

I do not believe political position alone is grounds for platform enforcement. The relevant integrity question is whether deceptive or coordinated accounts are artificially amplifying celebrity reach — especially through personas designed to appear young — and whether that activity is being used to manufacture social proof or parasocial engagement among younger users.

The war context makes this worth scrutiny, but the requested review is behavior-based. UN human-rights monitoring continues to document child casualties from Russia's ongoing attacks on Ukraine; in July 2026 alone, 17 children were killed and 166 injured, the highest monthly child-casualty total since April 2022. I therefore believe Meta should apply heightened care where public figures who support the war are being artificially amplified into youth-facing recommendation spaces through potentially deceptive personas.
