**Suggested subject:** Threads Integrity Review Request: Possible Managed-Persona Seeding Followed by Organic Amplification

To Meta Integrity / Threat Disruption / Threads Safety,

I am an independent researcher in Ukraine asking Meta to review a current Threads integrity pattern that may involve coordinated or managed persona seeding, deceptive SMM activity, and subsequent organic algorithmic amplification.

This report is **not primarily about Nikolay Baskov or Dima Bilan**. They are observable anchor accounts in a broader engagement pattern. I am also **not requesting enforcement because of anyone's political views or lawful speech**. I am asking for a behavior- and infrastructure-based integrity review.

## Core hypothesis: managed seeding may be converting into genuine organic virality

The public-facing mechanism I am asking Meta to test is:

`POSSIBLE MANAGED / PRE-POSITIONED PERSONAS`
→ `APPARENT RANDOM CELEBRITY INTERACTION`
→ `CELEBRITY REPLY`
→ `SOCIAL PROOF / SCREENSHOT / REPOST`
→ `GENUINE USERS IMITATE THE BEHAVIOR`
→ `MORE MENTIONS AND ENGAGEMENT`
→ `RECOMMENDATION / DISCOVERY SYSTEMS RECEIVE STRONGER ORGANIC SIGNALS`
→ `THE ORIGINAL SEED BECOMES DIFFICULT TO DISTINGUISH FROM NORMAL VIRALITY FROM THE OUTSIDE`

The critical question is therefore not simply whether suspicious accounts exist. It is whether an **early seed cohort** of apparently ordinary users was managed or coordinated, while a later cohort consists largely of genuine independent users who copied the behavior after seeing celebrity replies and social proof.

If that transition occurred, a small artificial or managed seed could create a much larger authentic engagement cascade without requiring a large botnet to remain active throughout the campaign.

## Observable Threads pattern

In 2026, a visible celebrity-reply ecosystem formed around Baskov, Bilan and other Russian celebrity accounts. Public posts repeatedly used the possibility of receiving a direct celebrity reply as a hook: users asked whether Baskov or Bilan would answer them, made conditional challenges based on receiving a reply, reposted celebrity responses, and encouraged others to repeat the behavior.

Public reporting and commentary also described a broader wave involving other Russian celebrities and repeatedly speculated that the volume and style of activity were being managed by SMM/PR teams. That public suspicion is **not evidence that one operator controls the accounts**, but it establishes a legitimate platform-side question about coordination and management relationships.

Within this broader wave, I observed a cluster of accounts presenting themselves as teenage or very young women, roughly late-teen / young-adult personas, repeatedly participating in or amplifying the reply-bait mechanism. I cannot verify the real age, gender or identity of those account operators from outside Meta. I am deliberately treating those characteristics as **persona presentation**, not established demographics.

## Why the early-cohort / late-cohort distinction matters

From public data alone, once genuine users begin copying the behavior, the campaign can look fully organic. Meta is uniquely positioned to test whether the first high-impact interactions differ materially from later participants.

I therefore ask Meta to compare:

- the earliest accounts receiving or manufacturing socially salient celebrity replies;
- the first accounts reposting or showcasing those replies as proof of celebrity accessibility;
- later users who independently imitate the mechanic;
- matched control cohorts from ordinary celebrity virality on Threads.

A particularly important falsifiable pattern would be:

`EARLY COHORT SHOWS SHARED MANAGEMENT / INFRASTRUCTURE`
while
`LATE COHORT IS PREDOMINANTLY INDEPENDENT`

That result would support a **platform seeding → organic cascade** mechanism. If both early and late cohorts are independent and behave like matched organic celebrity controls, that would weaken or falsify my concern.

## Current common-operator question

Public evidence confirms that Baskov and Bilan participated in the same Threads engagement ecosystem, interacted with one another, used closely aligned reply-bait / meme tactics, and were part of a broader celebrity participation wave. Public evidence does **not** currently establish one named SMM company, one common manager, one shared contract or one operator controlling both accounts.

That is precisely why this is a platform-side attribution question rather than a public accusation.

Meta can determine whether the relevant celebrity accounts and the apparent seed-persona accounts share managed-account infrastructure, access operators, devices, business relationships, contractors or other non-public relationships.

## Youth-coded persona and parasocial significance

The youth-coded persona layer matters because direct celebrity interaction can have unusually strong social-proof and parasocial effects, especially for younger audiences. Peer-reviewed research has associated direct celebrity social-media interactions such as replies or retweets with stronger parasocial relationships among adolescents, and more recent work describes influencers as potentially functioning as digital super-peers in adolescent information environments.

This does **not** establish that minors were targeted, that the observed accounts are actually minors, or that a political influence transition occurred. It means that if a managed persona-seeding network is present, Meta should evaluate the age/presentation and audience-distribution dimensions rather than treating the pattern as ordinary celebrity marketing by default.

## Relevant precedent class

Meta has previously documented Russia-linked coordinated inauthentic behavior using fake personas and non-political content, including lifestyle, fashion, celebrity and entertainment themes, to build audiences or engagement. U.S. authorities have separately documented Russian government-backed fake-persona / bot-farm operations used to distribute narratives internationally.

These precedents do **not** prove that the current Threads pattern is Russian state-directed. They establish that fake-persona audience seeding, engagement manufacture and apparently non-political audience building are real tactic classes worth checking when a current behavioral lead resembles parts of that pattern.

## Political context is secondary, not the enforcement basis

Baskov has publicly supported Russia's war against Ukraine. Bilan has also participated in public support activity connected with the war and has a longstanding youth-facing public profile.

I am not asserting:

- that their political views prove coordinated inauthentic behavior;
- that their Threads activity is directed by the Russian state;
- that the observed youth-coded accounts are fake;
- that actual minors were deliberately targeted;
- that all celebrity replies were pre-arranged;
- that every user participating in the trend is coordinated;
- that Meta intentionally amplified a political operation.

The political context becomes relevant **only if** Meta independently finds a hidden coordination structure, because such a finding would change the potential influence significance of what otherwise might be ordinary commercial SMM.

## Falsification contract

I am explicitly asking Meta to try to falsify this hypothesis.

The concern should be weakened or rejected if Meta finds that:

- the early reply/repost cohort consists of unrelated authentic users with independent histories;
- there is no meaningful shared management, device, network, recovery, contractor, payment or business relationship;
- engagement timing and recommendation exposure match normal celebrity virality controls;
- supposed seed accounts do not recur across celebrity accounts in a statistically or behaviorally unusual way;
- no deceptive identities, coordinated account management or artificial amplification are present.

The concern would strengthen if Meta finds that apparently independent early participants share operators, infrastructure, account-management relationships or pre-arranged interaction patterns, followed by a later wave dominated by unrelated genuine users.

## Why I am asking Meta rather than attempting external attribution

I am intentionally not presenting an externally reconstructed operator graph as proof. These interactions occurred inside Meta's own ecosystem. Meta has access to account creation, login, device, management, recommendation, enforcement and cross-product telemetry unavailable to an outside researcher.

An external observer can identify a repeatable public-facing mechanism. Meta can determine whether the mechanism began organically or whether the earliest interactions were seeded through managed personas.

My request is therefore simple:

**Please test whether the apparent celebrity-to-random-user interaction wave contains an early coordinated or managed seed layer that later converted into genuine organic amplification. If it does not, that result should falsify the hypothesis. If it does, please treat the coordinating structure as the integrity issue rather than the later genuine users who copied the behavior.**
