# DRAFT — ODNI / Foreign Malign Influence Center

**Status:** NOT SENT

**Suggested subject:** Strategic Early Warning: Public-Network Control Infrastructure as an Enabler of Foreign Malign Influence

To the Office of the Director of National Intelligence / Foreign Malign Influence Center,

I am submitting an unclassified strategic-warning hypothesis supported by open-source evidence and explicit falsifiers. The concern is not that a global Russian TSPU operation has been proven. It has not. The concern is that several independently documented capabilities could form a high-impact enabling chain if an additional, currently unproven, external-control bridge exists.

The chain is:

**historical control of information distribution during conflict**

+ **modern centrally controlled TSPU denial/filtering capability inside Russia**

+ **exported Russian SORM/DPI surveillance infrastructure abroad**

+ **persistent Russian foreign malign influence operations**

= a plausible future mechanism for **communications denial, information isolation, and amplified influence** against foreign populations and U.S. interests.

## What is established

First, Russian and Russian-backed forces have repeatedly treated information distribution as a strategic terrain. In Crimea and parts of Donetsk and Luhansk in 2014, broadcast/transmission infrastructure was seized and Ukrainian/local programming was removed or replaced with Russian/aligned sources. After the 2022 invasion, the mechanism expanded to Internet routing: Cloudflare measured occupied Ukrainian networks whose upstream connectivity shifted through Russian networks, causing users to inherit Russian network-control conditions.

Second, Russia now possesses a modern programmable public-network control architecture. Independent measurement work by Censored Planet characterized TSPU as pervasive, in-path and centrally controlled across privately owned Russian ISPs. FOCI 2026 documented large-scale SNI-dependent QUIC censorship, demonstrating substantial protocol-policy flexibility. Human Rights Watch has documented escalating TSPU-enabled censorship, isolation and shutdown behavior.

Third, Russia has demonstrated a crisis mode beyond ordinary blocking: mobile Internet restriction combined with an approved-service allowlist. Operationally, this creates the possibility of denying ordinary external communications while preserving selected domestic services. For strategic warning purposes, that is more significant than a simple total outage because it can alter both communications availability and the relative composition of the information environment.

Fourth, Russian SORM/surveillance technology has proliferated abroad. Recorded Future / Insikt Group identified at least eight Russian SORM providers exporting into Central Asia and Latin America and at least fifteen likely telecom customers. The report assesses that Russian-component foreign systems may create Russian/vendor access risk, including concerns about backdoors and manufacturer access, but explicitly states that the degree of Moscow's actual access is unclear.

Fifth, the United States already assesses Russian foreign malign influence as a persistent threat. The FBI, ODNI, CISA, DOJ and State Department have publicly documented Russian influence activities designed to shape foreign and U.S. attitudes, sow discord, weaken support for Ukraine and advance Russian strategic narratives.

## What is not established

My open-source review does **not** establish:

- Roskomnadzor-operated TSPU nodes in sovereign third countries;
- Russian state remote command of sovereign foreign SORM/DPI deployments;
- a synchronized multi-country activation;
- a specific transfer of North Korean censorship methods into TSPU;
- or that TSPU was originally designed as an offensive battlefield weapon.

These are precisely the missing collection questions.

## Why the combination matters

The strongest historical lesson from Crimea, Donbas and later occupied Ukrainian networks is not simply that propaganda exists. It is that control of the **distribution layer** changes which information and communications are physically available before persuasion is considered.

A TSPU-like system adds a digital version of that leverage. In a severe mode, a controller can potentially deny ordinary public Internet, preserve an allowlist, selectively block external services, and observe or shape whatever public-network traffic remains. Propaganda then becomes a downstream exploitation layer operating inside an artificially narrowed information environment.

For the United States, the first-order strategic effect may occur **outside U.S. infrastructure**. U.S. services can remain healthy while foreign populations, partners, journalists, companies, civil society, or U.S. personnel abroad lose access to them. U.S. service-side dashboards can therefore remain green while U.S. information reach and partner communications degrade geographically.

## Proposed FMIC collection / warning question

The central question is:

**Does any foreign deployment create a shared or Russian-accessible control plane capable of changing filtering/allowlist policy across multiple telecom environments?**

A high-confidence warning should require a composite signature, not narrative similarity:

- selective foreign-vantage loss of U.S./open services;
- approved/local services remaining materially healthier;
- reproducible DPI/TSPU-like protocol behavior or Russian-origin device/control-stack evidence;
- path/device localization;
- provider-side healthy controls;
- independent replication;
- and shared external command, vendor-access or control-plane linkage before Russian attribution.

If the hypothesis is wrong, candidate countries should show independent national policy timing, unrelated technical fingerprints, no common command infrastructure, and no correlation with Russian vendor/control access. Those outcomes should be treated as falsification, not explained away.

FMIC's public mission explicitly includes managing collection and analysis resources, identifying gaps, building external partnerships, and providing strategic warning on foreign adversary capabilities, intentions and operations. This appears to be the most appropriate destination for the integrated question above.

I have assembled an open-source evidence package containing the historical mechanism, technical TSPU literature, foreign deployment graph, corrections where our own hypothesis weakened, negative controls, and recipient-specific claim ceilings.
