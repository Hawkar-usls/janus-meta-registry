To the National Counterintelligence and Security Center / Office of the Director of National Intelligence,

Please route this unclassified open-source research submission internally to the current ODNI component or components responsible for foreign malign influence, integrated strategic warning and counterintelligence, including NCSC, Mission Integration and the National Intelligence Council, and FMIC if that function remains separately operational.

I am an independent researcher in Ukraine submitting a strategic-warning hypothesis built from public evidence, negative controls and explicit falsifiers. I am **not** alleging that a current global Russian TSPU operation has been proven. It has not. I am also **not** alleging that a current Threads celebrity-engagement pattern has been proven to be Russian state-directed. It has not.

The intelligence question is whether several separately demonstrated capabilities could become substantially more powerful if they were ever connected by a shared external control, timing or operator layer.

## Executive assessment

The established capability stack is:

**historical control of information-distribution infrastructure during conflict**

+ **modern centrally programmable Russian TSPU filtering / denial capability**

+ **repeated regional mobile shutdowns and an officially confirmed deny-except-allowlist mode**

+ **export of Russian-origin SORM / DPI / lawful-intercept technology to foreign telecom environments**

+ **documented Russian state-linked fake-persona, bot-farm and foreign influence operations**

= a plausible future mechanism for **communications denial, information isolation, audience pre-positioning, source substitution and amplified influence** against foreign populations and U.S. interests.

The missing bridge is critical: public evidence does **not** currently establish Russian remote command of sovereign foreign deployments, synchronized multi-country activation, or coupling between TSPU-like network control and the current Threads lead. Those are collection questions, not conclusions.

## 1. Historical distribution control is well established

Across multiple conflicts, Russian or Russia-backed forces have repeatedly treated information distribution as strategic terrain. The strongest documented cases include Crimea and Donbas in 2014, where broadcast/transmission infrastructure was seized and Ukrainian sources were replaced with Russian or aligned programming, and occupied Ukrainian territories after 2022, where broadcast assets were seized while Internet connectivity in multiple occupied networks was rerouted through Russian upstreams.

This is not presented as proof of one standing doctrine across every conflict. There are negative controls: Georgia 2008 and Syria show intense information warfare without the same clean tower-seize-and-replace sequence, and some historical cases involve neutralization rather than takeover. The defensible functional pattern is narrower: **control of the distribution layer can precede or accompany control of the accessible information environment.**

## 2. Russia now has a programmable public-network control architecture

Published technical work characterizes TSPU as pervasive, in-path and centrally controlled across Russian networks. Research on QUIC censorship demonstrates protocol-selective and SNI-dependent policy behavior. Russian practice also includes repeated regional mobile Internet restrictions and, by 2025-2026, an officially acknowledged mobile **deny-except-allowlist** mode in which selected services remain available while ordinary mobile Internet is restricted.

That matters because the strategic effect is not merely surveillance. For users whose communications depend on the controlled public-network path, the first-order effect can be **denial**, while approved local services remain reachable.

This claim is bounded: mobile shutdown evidence does not prove loss of every fixed-line, satellite, tactical-radio or dedicated communications path.

## 3. The Russian control surface reaches Russian-side cross-border infrastructure, but foreign TSPU deployment is not established

Russian official statements and network-management rules support the conclusion that Russian control architecture includes cross-border nodes and border-crossing lines on the Russian side. Measurements also show TSPU affecting international traffic originating inside Russia.

This does **not** establish Roskomnadzor-operated TSPU nodes in sovereign third countries or direct censorship of arbitrary foreign-origin traffic outside Russia. Any external attribution should require path localization, paired foreign/control measurements, reproducible device or policy fingerprints, independent replication and evidence of a shared external control plane.

## 4. Russian-origin surveillance and DPI technology is already present abroad

Open-source research has identified Russian SORM, DPI and lawful-intercept vendors and telecom customers in multiple foreign countries, including parts of Central Asia and Latin America. This establishes an exported capability substrate.

It does **not** prove Moscow has live operational command over those systems. Vendor access risk, Russian-manufactured components and local censorship are analytically distinct from Russian state remote control. That distinction is central to this submission.

The highest-value technical question is therefore architectural: **does any foreign deployment expose a Russian-accessible control plane capable of changing filtering, routing, interception or allowlist policy, and can that be independently demonstrated?**

## 5. Russia-linked fake-persona and influence operations are independently confirmed

Meta and U.S. authorities have repeatedly documented Russia-linked coordinated inauthentic behavior, fake personas, bot farms, influencers, paid amplification and audience-building activity. In 2024, DOJ/FBI disrupted a Russian government-operated AI-enhanced bot farm using 968 fictitious social-media accounts.

Meta has also documented Russia-linked operations that used non-political themes such as lifestyle, fashion, celebrity gossip and famous personalities to grow audiences or engagement. Those precedents do not attribute any current Threads account. They establish that **non-political audience seeding through managed personas is a real tactic class**, not a hypothetical invention.

## 6. Current platform lead: possible seeding followed by an organic cascade

A highly visible 2026 Threads engagement wave involving Russian celebrities including Nikolai Baskov and Dima Bilan is publicly observable. Users repeatedly seek celebrity replies, turn the replies into social proof, repost them and encourage further imitation. Public commentary also frequently suspected SMM/PR management because of the volume and style of the activity.

My narrower observation is an **unattributed lead**: some early participants present as teenage or very young women and appear repeatedly around the reply-bait / celebrity-accessibility mechanic. I cannot establish their real age, identity, operator or state affiliation from outside Meta.

The platform hypothesis separately submitted to Meta is:

`POSSIBLE MANAGED / PRE-POSITIONED PERSONAS`
→ `APPARENT RANDOM CELEBRITY INTERACTION`
→ `CELEBRITY REPLY`
→ `SOCIAL PROOF`
→ `GENUINE USER IMITATION`
→ `ORGANIC RECOMMENDATION / DISCOVERY AMPLIFICATION`

The decisive discriminator is whether the **early seed cohort** shows shared management or infrastructure while the later amplifying population is predominantly independent. That result would support a seed-to-organic mechanism in which a small managed nucleus triggers a much larger genuine cascade. If both cohorts are independent and match normal celebrity-virality controls, the concern should be weakened or rejected.

Public evidence does **not** currently establish that Baskov and Bilan share one SMM company, one operator or one state-linked control structure. That remains a platform-side attribution question.

## 7. Youth-coded personas are relevant as a mechanism question, not an accusation

Peer-reviewed work has associated direct celebrity social-media interactions such as replies or retweets with stronger parasocial relationships among adolescents, and newer work describes influencers as potential digital super-peers in adolescent information environments.

This does **not** establish that actual minors were targeted, that the observed personas are minors, or that a later political influence transition occurred. It is simply a reason to ask the platform to test actual audience and account-provenance data rather than infer age from profile appearance.

## 8. Secondary early-warning indicators from the longitudinal research

The broader research branch tracks several additional indicators that are useful for attribution and warning but are **not used as standalone proof**:

- **technical blame shifting:** recurrent explanations that attribute Russian users' loss or degradation of Western services to Google, Western censorship, platform exit or infrastructure failure even when independent network evidence may indicate domestic policy intervention;
- **phrase lineage:** repeated rare technical causal templates across official statements and user discussions, while distinguishing quotation/syndication from independent restatement;
- **old-account repurposing:** account age becomes informative only when combined with dormancy, topic pivots, identity changes, synchronized reactivation or shared media/phrase lineage;
- **old-media context substitution:** historically documented reuse of older footage or assets under a new date, place or event claim;
- **semantic-function shift:** a technically true fact can later be reused as an exculpatory or political explanation, so chronology and function should be tracked separately;
- **pre-existing narrative inventories:** recurring U.S./NATO-war and Western-decline themes may provide familiar frames during a future isolation event, but their persistence is not evidence that a technical event was preplanned around them.

These indicators are deliberately kept below operator/state-attribution threshold unless joined by technical, organizational or platform evidence.

## 9. Why the potential convergence matters to U.S. interests

The strategic risk is not limited to a direct attack on U.S. domestic infrastructure.

A foreign population could lose reliable access to U.S. media, cloud, developer, communications and independent information sources while those U.S. services remain technically healthy. At the same time, selected local services could remain available, and managed or socially amplified content could gain relative visibility inside the surviving information environment.

The result could be:

**U.S. services remain green**
→ **foreign reachability to them degrades**
→ **local/approved substitutes remain healthier**
→ **the accessible information mix narrows**
→ **pre-seeded or socially amplified narratives gain relative share**
→ **outside observers receive a slower or less representative picture of both network conditions and public attitudes**

The correct claim is **degraded or delayed observability**, not that the United States would become blind. Embassies, intelligence collection, providers, measurement platforms, diaspora communities and alternate communications remain important controls.

## 10. Proposed collection questions

1. **Foreign control plane:** Do any foreign telecom deployments using Russian-origin interception/DPI technology expose a Russian-accessible management plane capable of changing filtering, routing or allowlist policy?
2. **Foreign-vantage asymmetry:** Do U.S./open services become unreachable across selected foreign ASes while matched U.S.-side health and local substitute services remain normal?
3. **Cross-country synchronization:** Do semantically similar filtering or allowlist policies activate across multiple countries or operators within a narrow window beyond their normal independent-policy baseline?
4. **Device/path localization:** Can DNS, TLS/QUIC, reset behavior, TTL/path changes, BGP/AS-path evidence or other fingerprints localize enforcement to a specific controlled segment?
5. **Platform attribution:** Do current fake-persona or audience-seeding clusters overlap technically or organizationally with known RT/IRA/FSB/Kremlin-linked influence infrastructure?
6. **Seed-to-organic transition:** Does an early managed cohort precede a later predominantly independent cascade, and does this pattern recur across celebrity accounts, languages or countries?
7. **Narrative coupling:** Do audience-seeding or blame-shifting narratives intensify around independently measured access restrictions, after controlling for ordinary news propagation and genuine provider failures?
8. **Longitudinal infrastructure:** Do dormant or repurposed accounts, repeated rare phrase templates or reused media assets reveal shared campaign lineage only after independent operator evidence is added?

## 11. Explicit falsifiers and claim ceiling

The hypothesis should be weakened or rejected when evidence shows:

- independent national policy timing rather than synchronized control;
- normal provider failures, sanctions or product restrictions explaining service loss;
- authentic unrelated fan accounts and ordinary commercial SMM explaining platform engagement;
- no common operator, device, payment, business or infrastructure relationship in the early seed cohort;
- Russian-origin foreign equipment operating solely under local sovereign control with no Russian-accessible management layer;
- no path-localized filtering signature or no difference from matched control routes;
- no relationship between access restrictions and narrative/platform activity beyond normal news cycles.

My review does **not** establish:

- Roskomnadzor-operated TSPU nodes in sovereign third countries;
- Russian state remote command of sovereign foreign SORM/DPI deployments;
- synchronized multi-country TSPU-like activation;
- a current global Russian information-curtain operation;
- state direction of the current Threads celebrity wave;
- one common SMM operator behind Baskov and Bilan;
- that the observed youth-coded personas are fake or are actually minors;
- deliberate targeting of actual minors;
- that repeated phrases alone identify bots or state assets;
- that old accounts or old media are suspicious without chronology/context evidence;
- that every claim blaming Google or Western restrictions is false;
- specific DPRK censorship-method transfer into TSPU;
- or that TSPU was purpose-built as a universal battlefield C2 weapon.
