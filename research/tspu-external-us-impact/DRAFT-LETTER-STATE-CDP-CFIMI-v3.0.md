**Suggested subject:** Partner-Resilience Warning: Network Isolation, Audience Seeding, and Foreign Information Manipulation

To the U.S. Department of State — Bureau of Cyberspace and Digital Policy and Counter Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference personnel,

I am submitting an unclassified early-warning concern about the intersection of authoritarian Internet control, public-network resilience and foreign state information manipulation.

The core concern is not that a global Russian TSPU system has been proven outside Russia. It has not. The concern is that Russia has separately demonstrated several capabilities which, if ever combined abroad through a shared control or coordination layer, could materially reduce U.S. information reach and partner-country resilience.

## Historical and technical basis

Russia and Russian-backed forces have repeatedly treated information distribution as strategic terrain. Crimea and parts of Donetsk and Luhansk in 2014 provide documented examples of broadcast/transmission seizure, competing-source removal and Russian/aligned source substitution. Russia also continued striking Ukrainian broadcast infrastructure after the 2022 full-scale invasion. In parallel, Cloudflare independently measured occupied Ukrainian networks being rerouted through Russian upstreams, demonstrating a modern Internet-path analogue of distribution control.

Inside Russia, TSPU is now a widely deployed, in-path and centrally controlled public-network policy system. Independent technical research has documented protocol-selective censorship, while Human Rights Watch has documented expanding isolation/shutdown capability. By 2025-2026 Russian authorities also operated a mobile **deny-except-allowlist** regime in which selected approved services remain reachable during broader mobile Internet restrictions.

For foreign policy, this creates a critical asymmetry: U.S. media, U.S. government information, international platforms, independent journalism or ordinary cross-border communications could become unreachable from an affected network while selected domestic services remain functional. U.S.-side systems could remain entirely healthy.

Russian SORM/surveillance technology has also proliferated into foreign telecom environments. Recorded Future / Insikt Group identified multiple Russian vendors and likely foreign telecom customers and assessed that Russian-component deployments may create vendor/Russian access risk, while explicitly noting that the degree of Moscow's actual access is unclear. This is a resilience and supply-chain concern, not proof of Russian command.

## Influence-distribution precedent

Russian social-media influence operations are also independently documented. DOJ/FBI disrupted a Russian government-operated AI-enhanced bot farm using fictitious personas to spread Russian-government narratives in the United States and abroad. Meta has repeatedly removed Russia-linked coordinated inauthentic networks and documented tactics including fake personas, engagement amplification and audience building through non-political themes such as lifestyle, fashion, celebrity gossip and famous personalities.

This matters because an influence operation does not need to begin with overt political messaging. It can first build familiarity, reach and social trust.

## Current Threads lead — celebrity reply bait

A highly visible March 2026 Threads wave around Nikolai Baskov, Dima Bilan and other Russian celebrities is independently documented. Public reporting confirms repeated celebrity/account-manager replies to apparently random users; the behavior became a platform-wide meme, including jokes that ordinary users could post and receive a reply from Baskov or Bilan. Contemporary users also publicly suspected PR/SMM management.

I have observed a more specific pattern that I cannot independently attribute from public search alone: accounts presenting as teenage/young women, approximately 15-18+ in persona, repeatedly promoting Baskov and Bilan around the possibility of receiving a personal reply. Apparent replies become screenshots/social proof and encourage additional users to imitate the behavior, creating a flashmob-like engagement loop.

I treat this as an **unattributed current lead**, not evidence of a Russian state campaign. The personas may be ordinary fans, commercial SMM or fabricated identities; their actual age and operator are unknown.

The mechanism nonetheless deserves resilience attention. Peer-reviewed research indicates that adolescents who receive direct celebrity replies or retweets report stronger parasocial relationships, and 2026 longitudinal research describes influencers as potential "digital super peers" in adolescent socio-political information environments. This supports the general vulnerability mechanism without proving deliberate targeting of minors in the current case.

## Background narrative inventory — not proof of technical coordination

The supporting package preserves a long-running Russian narrative inventory that should be treated as **context, not attribution evidence**. Senior Russian officials and state/pro-government media have repeatedly framed the war as a confrontation with the United States, NATO or the collective West. Separately, Russian official/state-aligned discourse has for roughly fifteen years repeatedly used U.S. debt milestones and broader Western economic, political and civilizational decline themes.

The analytical boundary is important: U.S. federal debt is a real fiscal challenge, and reporting debt milestones is not inherently propaganda. Narrative prevalence likewise does not prove persuasion. The relevance is that a technically narrowed information environment would not begin empty; established narratives already exist and can be measured for changes in relative exposure if external/open sources become less reachable.

## Why the combination matters

The strategic risk is a two-lane environment:

**Technical lane:** external/open information and communications become selectively less reachable through deny/allowlist/filtering/path control.

**Influence lane:** fake personas, celebrity/lifestyle audience seeding, engagement amplification and socially reinforced narratives operate inside the remaining reachable platforms.

If these lanes were ever coordinated, technical isolation could increase the relative share of state-aligned or manipulated information without requiring users to consciously choose a new source. The United States could lose information reach abroad even while its domestic services remain operational.

There is currently **no evidence establishing that the Threads celebrity wave and TSPU activity are part of a single operation**. The point of this submission is to make that coupling a measurable early-warning question rather than an assumption.

## Partner-resilience indicators

I suggest monitoring:

- simultaneous foreign-vantage testing of U.S./open services and approved/local substitutes;
- mobile and fixed networks separately;
- DNS/TLS/QUIC/basic reachability plus AS-path observations;
- Russian-origin surveillance/control vendors without equating vendor origin with Russian command;
- platform-level evidence of coordinated fake personas, common operator infrastructure or synchronized engagement patterns;
- disproportionate reach into adolescent audiences only when supported by platform demographic data, not profile appearance;
- transition from non-political audience-building into coordinated political/war narratives;
- changes in exposure to pre-existing U.S./NATO-enemy and Western-decline narratives after technical access changes;
- and common external control evidence before attributing cross-country synchronization to Russia.

Authentic fan activity, ordinary celebrity virality, independent host-government censorship or commercial SMM should remain active falsifiers.

The strongest supported position is therefore:

**Russia has repeatedly integrated information-distribution control with conflict influence operations, possesses a sophisticated centrally controlled domestic network-isolation capability, exports adjacent surveillance technologies, and operates documented fake-persona/bot influence campaigns. A coordinated external TSPU-plus-platform influence system is not proven, but the precursor signatures are concrete enough to justify partner-resilience monitoring.**
