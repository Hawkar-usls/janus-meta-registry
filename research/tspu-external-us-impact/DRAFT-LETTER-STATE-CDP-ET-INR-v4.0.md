**Suggested subject:** Unclassified Partner-Resilience Warning — Network Isolation, Source Substitution, and Foreign Information Manipulation

To the U.S. Department of State,

Please route this unclassified open-source research submission to the Bureau of Cyberspace and Digital Policy (especially International Information and Communications Policy, Digital Freedom, and International Cyberspace Security), the Bureau of Emerging Threats / Office of Threat Assessment, the Bureau of Intelligence and Research / Office of Cyber Affairs and Emerging Technology, and any current Department component responsible for foreign information manipulation and interference.

I am an independent researcher in Ukraine submitting a partner-resilience and early-warning concern. The central proposition is **not** that a global Russian TSPU operation outside Russia has been proven. It has not. Nor do I claim that a current social-media engagement pattern has been proven to be Russian state-directed. It has not.

The concern is narrower and actionable for diplomacy: Russia has separately demonstrated several capabilities that, if combined abroad through a shared control, vendor-access, policy, timing, or operator layer, could reduce foreign populations' access to U.S./open information while increasing the relative reach of locally controlled or socially amplified substitute sources.

## Executive assessment

The established capability stack is:

**historical control of information-distribution infrastructure during conflict**

+ **modern centrally programmable TSPU filtering / denial inside Russia**

+ **repeated regional mobile restrictions and an officially acknowledged mobile deny-except-allowlist mode**

+ **export of Russian-origin SORM / DPI / lawful-intercept technology into foreign telecom environments**

+ **documented Russian state-linked fake-persona, bot-farm, and foreign influence operations**

= a plausible future pathway to **information isolation + source substitution + audience pre-positioning + amplified influence**.

The missing bridge is the most important part of the analysis. Public evidence does **not** currently establish Russian remote command of sovereign foreign deployments, synchronized multi-country activation, or operational coupling between TSPU-like network control and any current platform campaign. Those remain collection and resilience questions, not conclusions.

## 1. Information-distribution control is a documented conflict mechanism

Russian and Russia-backed forces have repeatedly treated information distribution as strategic terrain. In Crimea and Donbas in 2014, broadcast/transmission infrastructure was seized and Ukrainian sources were removed or replaced with Russian/aligned programming. After the 2022 full-scale invasion, occupied Ukrainian broadcast assets were seized while Cloudflare independently measured multiple occupied Ukrainian networks being rerouted through Russian upstreams.

This does not prove one immutable doctrine across every conflict. Georgia 2008, Syria, and some Chechen-war cases provide useful controls where information warfare existed without the same clean seize-and-replace sequence. The defensible functional observation is narrower:

**control of the distribution layer can precede or accompany control of the accessible information environment.**

For partner resilience, that historical mechanism matters more than any single propaganda narrative.

## 2. TSPU demonstrates programmable denial and selective-access capability

Independent technical research characterizes Russia's TSPU system as widely deployed, in-path, and centrally controlled across Russian networks. Published measurements show protocol-selective policy behavior, including SNI-dependent QUIC censorship.

Russian practice also includes repeated regional mobile Internet restrictions and, by 2025-2026, an officially acknowledged mobile **deny-except-allowlist** mode in which approved services remain reachable during broader restrictions.

For an affected population this creates a critical asymmetry:

**U.S./open services may remain technically healthy**
→ **foreign users lose reliable reachability to them**
→ **selected local/approved services remain available**.

The claim is deliberately bounded. Mobile shutdown evidence does not imply loss of every fixed-line, satellite, tactical-radio, or dedicated communications path.

## 3. Russian-side cross-border control is established; foreign TSPU nodes are not

Russian official statements and current network-management rules support the conclusion that the Russian control surface includes Russian-side cross-border nodes and border-crossing lines. Measurements also show TSPU affecting international traffic originating inside Russia.

This does **not** establish Roskomnadzor-operated TSPU nodes in sovereign third countries or direct censorship of arbitrary foreign-origin traffic outside Russia.

Any future external attribution should require a composite evidentiary gate: path localization, paired affected/control measurements, reproducible policy/device fingerprints, route controls, independent replication, and evidence of a common external command or management layer.

## 4. Russian-origin SORM/DPI technology abroad creates a partner-resilience collection surface

Open-source research has identified Russian SORM, DPI, and lawful-intercept vendors and telecom customers in multiple countries, including parts of Central Asia and Latin America.

This establishes an exported technology substrate. It does **not** establish live Russian-government command of those systems. Russian vendor origin, vendor support access, host-government censorship, and Russian state operational control are separate analytic categories.

The high-value diplomatic and technical question is therefore:

**Do any partner-country deployments expose a Russian-accessible remote-management or policy-control plane capable of changing filtering, routing, interception, or allowlist behavior?**

A negative answer is useful. If systems are locally controlled, independently audited, and operationally isolated from Russian access, that should reduce the concern rather than be discarded.

## 5. Foreign influence capability is separately established

U.S. authorities and major platforms have repeatedly documented Russia-linked fake-persona, bot-farm, paid amplification, influencer, and coordinated inauthentic behavior operations. In 2024 DOJ/FBI disrupted a Russian government-operated AI-enhanced bot farm using 968 fictitious social-media accounts.

Meta has also documented Russia-linked networks that used non-political themes such as lifestyle, fashion, celebrity gossip, and famous personalities for audience building or engagement.

Those precedents do not attribute any current account. They establish a narrower point relevant to resilience: **political influence infrastructure can be pre-positioned through non-political social mechanisms before overt messaging appears.**

## 6. Current platform lead: possible managed seeding followed by organic amplification

A highly visible 2026 Threads engagement wave involving Russian celebrities including Nikolai Baskov and Dima Bilan is publicly observable. Users seek celebrity replies, repost the replies as social proof, and encourage imitation. Public commentary also repeatedly suspected SMM/PR management because of the volume and style of the activity.

My narrower observation remains an **unattributed lead**: some early participants present as teenage or very young women and appear around the reply-bait / celebrity-accessibility mechanism. From outside Meta I cannot establish their real ages, identities, operators, or state affiliation.

The platform-side hypothesis, already sent separately to Meta, is:

`POSSIBLE MANAGED / PRE-POSITIONED PERSONAS`
→ `APPARENT RANDOM CELEBRITY INTERACTION`
→ `CELEBRITY REPLY`
→ `SOCIAL PROOF`
→ `GENUINE USER IMITATION`
→ `ORGANIC RECOMMENDATION / DISCOVERY AMPLIFICATION`

The strongest discriminator is whether an **early seed cohort** shares hidden management or infrastructure while the later amplifying population is predominantly independent. If both cohorts are independent and match ordinary celebrity-virality controls, the concern should be weakened or rejected.

This is relevant to State only as a **partner-resilience mechanism**, not as a request to police political speech or punish lawful viewpoints.

## 7. Why technical isolation can amplify information manipulation without changing U.S. service health

The strategic risk is not limited to a direct attack on U.S. domestic infrastructure.

A foreign population could lose reliable access to U.S. media, independent journalism, cloud/developer services, communications platforms, or circumvention tools while those U.S. services remain fully operational. If selected local services remain reachable, the accessible information mix narrows automatically.

The potential chain is:

**U.S./open sources remain healthy**
→ **foreign reachability selectively degrades**
→ **local/approved substitutes remain healthier**
→ **source diversity falls**
→ **pre-positioned or socially amplified narratives gain relative share**
→ **outside observers receive a slower or less representative picture of network conditions and public attitudes**.

The correct warning is **degraded or delayed observability**, not that the United States would become blind. Embassies, providers, measurement platforms, diaspora communities, civil society, journalists, alternate communications, and intelligence collection remain critical controls.

## 8. Longitudinal indicators that may help distinguish manipulation from ordinary failures

The research branch preserves additional indicators, none of which is treated as standalone attribution evidence:

- **technical blame shifting:** recurring explanations that attribute access degradation to Google, Western censorship, sanctions, platform exit, or infrastructure failure when independent measurements may indicate domestic policy intervention;
- **phrase lineage:** repeated rare causal templates across official statements and user discussions, while separating quotation/syndication from independent restatement;
- **old-account repurposing:** account age matters only when combined with dormancy, topic pivots, identity changes, synchronized reactivation, or shared media/phrase lineage;
- **old-media context substitution:** older footage or assets reused under a new date, location, or event claim;
- **semantic-function shift:** a technically true fact can later be reused as an exculpatory political explanation, so first appearance and later rhetorical function must be tracked separately;
- **pre-existing narrative inventories:** recurring U.S./NATO-war and Western-decline frames may provide familiar interpretive schemas during an isolation event, but persistence does not prove technical coordination or causal persuasion.

Real Western geoblocking, sanctions effects, platform moderation, genuine provider failures, and genuine cache/infrastructure degradation are mandatory negative controls.

## 9. Partner-resilience indicators for State consideration

I suggest a defensive, partner-facing baseline built around:

1. **Paired foreign-vantage measurements:** test U.S./open services and local/approved substitutes at the same time.
2. **Access-layer separation:** measure mobile, fixed, enterprise, satellite, and alternate paths separately rather than generalizing one outage to all communications.
3. **Path evidence:** preserve DNS, TLS/QUIC, reset behavior, TTL/path changes, BGP/AS-path data, and other localization evidence.
4. **Vendor/control-plane inventory:** distinguish product origin from actual remote management, support access, software update authority, and policy-control privileges.
5. **Independent-country baselines:** identify each partner's normal censorship/security policy timing before treating similarity as synchronization.
6. **Source-substitution monitoring:** document when local/state-aligned services are promoted or remain available as external sources degrade.
7. **Platform integrity liaison:** where lawful and appropriate, ask platforms to distinguish early coordinated seeding from later organic participation.
8. **Narrative timing:** compare information-manipulation activity against independently measured access events, not against rumor or complaint volume alone.
9. **Civil-society and embassy reporting:** combine technical probes with journalists, rights groups, businesses, researchers, and diplomatic reporting.
10. **Falsification-first review:** independent local policy, ordinary outages, authentic fan behavior, commercial SMM, and locally controlled foreign equipment should count against the Russian-coordination hypothesis when supported by evidence.

## 10. Why this fits current State structures

Current State organization material assigns CDP responsibility for international ICT policy, cyberspace security, digital freedom, digital information integrity, and resistance to repressive state-controlled practices in cyberspace. CDP/ICP also maintains liaison with foreign governments and interagency partners on international communications and information policy.

The Department's current organization codes also list the Bureau of Emerging Threats with an Office of Threat Assessment and Office of Cybersecurity, while INR's Office of Cyber Affairs and Emerging Technology produces all-source analysis on foreign-government cyber, telecommunications, and emerging-technology issues.

Public records regarding the former Counter Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference Hub are inconsistent: current State administrative codes still display the R/CFIMI symbol, while 2026 public court filings describe an earlier order to close the Hub. I therefore do not rely on R/CFIMI as a live intake endpoint and respectfully ask that this submission be routed to whatever current component, if any, retains the relevant foreign-information-manipulation function.

## 11. Claim ceiling and explicit falsifiers

My review does **not** establish:

- Roskomnadzor-operated TSPU nodes in sovereign third countries;
- Russian state remote command of sovereign foreign SORM/DPI deployments;
- synchronized multi-country TSPU-like activation;
- a current global Russian information-curtain operation;
- state direction of the current Threads celebrity wave;
- one common SMM operator behind Baskov and Bilan;
- that youth-coded personas are fake or actually minors;
- deliberate targeting of actual minors;
- bot/state attribution from repeated phrases alone;
- state attribution from a 2013/2014 account creation date;
- that every explanation blaming Google, Western restrictions, sanctions, or infrastructure failure is false;
- specific DPRK censorship-method transfer into TSPU;
- or universal denial of military communications.

The hypothesis should be weakened or rejected where evidence shows normal provider failure, independent national policy timing, locally controlled equipment without Russian remote access, no path-localized filtering, authentic unrelated users, ordinary commercial SMM, or no measurable coupling between access changes and influence activity.

## 12. Concrete request

I am not asking State to endorse an attribution. I am asking whether the Department considers the **network-isolation → source-substitution → influence-amplification** pathway sufficiently plausible to justify partner-country baselines and early-warning collection before a crisis.

If the answer is yes, the most useful next step would be to preserve a common operating picture across partner networks: who controls the relevant telecom policy planes, what U.S./open sources remain reachable during restrictions, what substitutes remain available, whether filtering signatures localize to a specific path, and whether information-manipulation activity changes in a way that survives matched controls.
