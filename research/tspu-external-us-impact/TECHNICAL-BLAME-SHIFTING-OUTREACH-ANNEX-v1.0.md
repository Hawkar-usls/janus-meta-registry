## Why this matters

A network-control capability has a second information effect beyond blocking itself: the actor imposing or benefiting from access degradation may attempt to shape **who users blame for the degradation**.

The relevant mechanism is:

`seed/maintain a technical explanation blaming the foreign platform or its infrastructure`

→ `foreign service becomes degraded or inaccessible`

→ `repeat the pre-existing explanation as the apparent cause`

→ `redirect user anger away from domestic filtering/policy infrastructure and toward the external platform/West`.

This is analytically distinct from ordinary censorship and from ordinary propaganda. It is **technical attribution manipulation**.

## Confirmed technical baseline: domestic filtering can break Google access

In April 2013, Google services became unavailable to some Rostelecom subscribers because of a software failure in Russian equipment that analyzed IP addresses for the federal blocking registry. Similar late-March access problems affected YouTube and other sites. This establishes an important baseline: loss of access to Google/YouTube inside Russia can originate in the domestic filtering layer rather than in Google infrastructure.

Source: https://ria.ru/20130404/930950133.html

## 2014 context — preserve as control, not proof

In March and July 2014, Russian state/state-aligned media prominently covered restrictions affecting RT on YouTube as actions of the foreign platform. These events should **not** be misrepresented as covert Russian blocking: in March Google itself acknowledged a technical error, while the July dispute involved copyright-related functionality restrictions.

Sources:
- https://rg.ru/2014/03/18/rt_youtube-site-anons.html
- https://rg.ru/2014/07/10/translatii.html

These cases are useful as context showing the salience of an external-platform-blocking narrative, but they are active negative controls against over-attribution.

## Strong public precedent: Russia's 2024 YouTube slowdown

In July 2024, Roskomnadzor and senior Russian officials publicly attributed deterioration of YouTube performance to Google's alleged failure to maintain Google Global Cache infrastructure after Google reduced operations in Russia.

Official-attribution reporting:
- https://www.interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/104600/
- https://www.reuters.com/technology/google-blame-slower-youtube-speeds-russia-says-senior-lawmaker-2024-07-26/

Independent traffic analysis and telecom-industry reporting, however, found evidence consistent with targeted throttling by Russian authorities rather than simultaneous failure of Google cache equipment. Subsequent industry reporting described severe YouTube rate limits communicated to providers.

Counterevidence:
- https://meduza.io/en/feature/2024/07/12/traffic-speeds-reveal-that-russia-s-youtube-slowdown-is-targeted-throttling-not-google-global-cache-equipment-failure
- https://meduza.io/en/feature/2024/08/06/russian-authorities-inform-local-internet-service-providers-that-most-youtube-access-is-now-capped-at-a-piddling-128-kbps

**Assessment:** this is the clearest public precedent in the current package for a mismatch between the official technical attribution (`Google infrastructure degradation`) and stronger independent evidence of deliberate domestic throttling.
