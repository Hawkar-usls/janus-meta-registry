Measure the eye. Keep interpretation separate.

The JANUS Wedjat line studies component-state representations, geometric relationships and museum-image regions of interest around the Egyptian Wedjat / Eye of Horus. The research is exploratory and control-oriented: visual recurrence or codebook-like structure is not treated as proof of an ancient hidden code, reading order or authorial intention.

Component-state space

Individual eye components and their combinations can be represented as explicit states, allowing exhaustive or controlled comparisons without assuming those states carried a historical code.

Museum-image provenance

Real-object analysis is separated from synthetic drawings. Eligibility audits track whether images are suitable for geometry or region-of-interest work and where provenance is incomplete.

Geometry and codebook stress tests

Subset, partition and composition probes ask whether apparent relationships survive explicit controls, rather than assuming a visually appealing correspondence is meaningful.

Why external review matters

Geometry can be measured internally, but historical interpretation depends on archaeological context, object provenance, periodization, writing conventions and specialist knowledge outside a purely computational pipeline.
